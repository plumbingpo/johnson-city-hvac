Keeping Your Nashville HVAC System Running Longer

The best way to avoid a mid-season breakdown is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners replace filters every one to three months, keep shrubs and grass trimmed two feet back from the outdoor condenser, rinse the condenser coil each spring, and have a professional tune-up twice a year, cooling in spring and heating in fall, so small problems get caught while they're still inexpensive.

The age of your equipment matters just as much. With regular service, a central air conditioner typically lasts 12 to 15 years, a furnace 15 to 20, and a heat pump 10 to 15. Once a system passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like rising energy bills, rooms that never reach the setpoint, or repeat service calls, it's smart to start planning a replacement. A planned replacement costs less and is far less stressful than an emergency swap during the first heat wave of summer.

Minor issues rarely stay small when they're ignored. Consider a weak capacitor: swapping it out is a quick, affordable repair, but if it's ignored for a season, it can burn out the compressor, and suddenly a minor fix has become a full system replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to keep our number in their phone and reach out at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and spares you a miserable night.
